Can pitch data catch a rigged pitch?

In 2025 two Cleveland pitchers were charged with selling individual pitches to bettors. Every alarm so far came from the betting side. We asked whether the pitch-tracking data itself gives a fixer away, and how many innocent pitchers an alarm like that would also catch.

Why our model lost: it looks for pitches that are strange for him, but a rigged ball in the dirt is a normal-looking miss, and 6 of the 18 were called strikes, fouls or swinging strikes, bets on speed or pitch type that leave no trace in location. Asking "is this one pitch suspicious?" is the wrong question.
03 · Result two

The pattern shows up in the rate.

The indictment says the rigged pitches were usually the first pitch of an at-bat, thrown in the dirt. So instead of judging single pitches, we track how often each pitcher does that every month and compare it with his own normal.

06 · Method & data

Code computes. Nothing guesses.

Data
  • Every MLB pitch 2023–25 from Baseball Savant (Statcast) via pybaseball. Real data, exact use case.
  • 18 named pitches from the Sept 18 2026 filing as reported by Sports Betting Dime and TSN, matched one-to-one to Statcast rows. Ortiz game dates from the DOJ release.
  • Nothing simulated or mocked.
Pitch model (v1)

For each pitch: how rare its location is for that pitch type and batter side, how often he picks that pitch in that count, and how unusual its speed is. Each is compared only with his earlier games (no look-ahead, tested), then combined with Fisher's method. Frozen at commit 5fa3b3e before evaluation.

Pattern monitor (v2)

Template from the indictment's own words: first pitch of an at-bat, taken for a ball, more than 30% of the zone height below the bottom. Each pitcher-month with 20+ first pitches is scored with a binomial tail test against his own rate in his other months, shrunk toward the league rate. Score = −log10 p.

Honest limitations
  • Only 18 labelled pitches, so intervals are wide.
  • We designed v2 after seeing the 18 labels. The league test is the honest check, because it is dominated by pitchers the template never saw.
  • No 2022 data, so early-2023 baselines lean on the league rate.
